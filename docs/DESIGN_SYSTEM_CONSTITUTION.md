# DESIGN SYSTEM CONSTITUTION

## Phase 6.2 — Visual Authority Principles

SufiPulse must visually project the structural authority it possesses. The design system rejects modern SaaS trends, startup templates, and generic admin dashboards. The visual language must communicate: **Wikipedia depth + Apple-level polish + Notion intelligence + Perplexity clarity + Financial Terminal seriousness.**

---

### 1. Typography (Museum-Grade)
* **Rule:** Text is the primary interface. It must be editorial, hyper-legible, and meticulously typeset.
* **Characteristics:** Use high-contrast, beautiful serif headers paired with clean, geometric sans-serif bodies. Line height and character spacing must be optimized for deep, immersive reading.

### 2. Layout (Content-Aware)
* **Rule:** The grid serves the content, not the other way around.
* **Characteristics:** Asymmetric and breathing. Layouts must adapt to the density of the entity. Do not force empty placeholder boxes if data is sparse. Use expansive whitespace to frame knowledge artifactually.

### 3. Motion (Purposeful)
* **Rule:** Animation is used strictly to reveal relationships, never to distract.
* **Characteristics:** Subtle fade-ins, gentle parallax on historical imagery, and smooth structural transitions when navigating the graph. Motion should feel like turning the pages of an ancient manuscript, refined for the AI era.

### 4. Cards (Knowledge Artifacts)
* **Rule:** Never use generic "blog" or "dashboard" cards.
* **Characteristics:** A card representing a Song or a Concept must feel like holding a physical Knowledge Artifact. They should feature elegant borders, delicate typography, and rich hover states that hint at the intelligence beneath.

### 5. Colors (Premium & Scholarly)
* **Rule:** The palette must be timeless.
* **Characteristics:** Deep ink blacks, parchment off-whites, and muted but deliberate accent colors (e.g., aged gold, deep lapis blue, terra cotta) that evoke historical manuscripts and premium scholarly journals. No neon, no generic "startup blue."
